# Pilot Source Ledger — Urban Heat Islands

**Research status:** Initial authoritative-source pass completed 10 November 2026.  
**Scope:** General explanatory claims for a short pilot. These sources do not establish local measurements for Chennai or any other specific city.

## Core sources

1. **U.S. Environmental Protection Agency — “What Are Heat Islands?”**  
   https://www.epa.gov/heatislands/what-are-heat-islands  
   Supports: definition; built surfaces absorbing and re-emitting heat; reduced vegetation; shade and evapotranspiration; urban geometry and airflow; human-generated waste heat; variability by weather and geography; surface versus atmospheric heat islands.

2. **U.S. Environmental Protection Agency — “Guide to Reducing Heat Islands.”**  
   https://www.epa.gov/heatislands/guide-reducing-heat-islands  
   Supports: vegetation and moisture loss as urban areas develop; material properties; possible mitigation strategies and their context-dependent benefits.

3. **NASA Science — “What Is an Urban Heat Island?”**  
   https://science.nasa.gov/kids/earth/what-is-an-urban-heat-island/  
   Supports: accessible explanation of urban/rural temperature differences; surface heat absorption and retention; transpiration; dark building materials and reflective alternatives.

4. **NASA Science — “Can you explain the urban heat island effect?”**  
   https://science.nasa.gov/climate-change/faq/can-you-explain-the-urban-heat-island-effect/  
   Supports: heat islands are local effects; their intensity differs between cities; urban heat island effects should not be conflated with global warming.

## Claim-to-source map

| Script claim | Primary support | Editorial treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Built surfaces absorb and release heat | EPA 1; NASA 3 | Explain as a general tendency, not a universal property of every material. |
| Heat can persist after sunset | EPA 1; NASA 3 | Show stored heat being released gradually; avoid implying all surfaces behave identically. |
| Vegetation cools through shade and evapotranspiration | EPA 1; NASA 3 | Briefly define the process in plain language. |
| Temperature varies within a city | EPA 1 | Use contrasting illustrative locations, not invented measured temperatures. |
| Vehicles, buildings and AC can add waste heat | EPA 1 | Present as one contributing factor among several. |
| Greening and reflective surfaces can help | EPA 2; NASA 3 | Say “can”; effectiveness depends on local conditions and design. |
| Heat islands vary and are not global warming | EPA 1; NASA 4 | Avoid universal numerical claims and global-climate confusion. |
