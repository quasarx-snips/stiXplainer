# stiXplainer Visual Style Bible

**Default, owner-editable visual target.** Source: the owner's uploaded audio-removed reference video. The supplied proxy is 256×144 at 30 fps and approximately 21:19; small line details are not treated as reliable.

## Visual identity
A warm, hand-drawn 2D cartoon explainer in which an original recurring stick-figure lead physically experiences the story while illustrated environments, objects, animals and diagrams make the explanation visible. The reference is visual inspiration only: never copy its exact character, frames, drawings, branding or assets.

## Characters
- Original lead: large pale circular head, minimal expressive face, thin dark limbs, organic dark outlines; default clothing is a clear blue top and blue/neutral trousers.
- Keep head proportions, eye/mouth placement, limb thickness and clothing consistent across scenes.
- Supporting figures use the same simple construction; distinguish them with clothing, hair, props and posture.
- Reusable expressions: neutral, curious, surprised, worried, confused, determined, amused, frightened, relieved.
- Reusable poses: walk, run, point, shrug, climb, carry, crouch, look up, stumble and react.
- Readable silhouettes at phone size. Designs must be original, not traced.

## Art and colour
- Slightly imperfect hand-drawn charcoal/near-black outlines.
- Simple cartoon shapes and flat fills; restrained shading; no glossy 3D.
- Small scene-specific palette: blue skies/water, greens, warm earth tones, yellow/orange fire/heat, navy night, off-white diagram backgrounds.
- Build complete illustrated locations with foreground/middle/background where useful. Use simple landmarks to establish place.

## Scene grammar
Alternate: (1) character action, (2) full environment, (3) explanatory object/animal/mechanism illustration, (4) clean diagram/timeline/comparison, (5) return to character for reaction or consequence. Use cuts, pans, push-ins, pull-backs, wipes, object reveals and drawing-on transitions when they explain a change of time, place, scale or idea.

## Text and composition
- 16:9, 1920×1080, 24 fps; compose for mobile readability.
- One dominant action or diagram per shot.
- Short hand-lettered labels/headings, arrows and callouts only when useful.
- Avoid paragraphs, giant title cards, persistent caption bars and slide-per-paragraph pacing.
- Subtitles are a separate synchronized SRT by default.

## Motion and storytelling
Animate body language, faces, relevant environmental details and diagram build order. Use anticipation, clear action and a readable settle. Start with a vivid question/problem, show the lead encountering it, explain cause and effect visually, then deliver a memorable payoff. Humour is welcome when appropriate. No static-scene padding.

## Audio
The reference is silent; it establishes no audio style. Project default remains warm British English male storyteller, selective sound effects and rights-cleared music. Never infer original dialogue or music from this clip.

## Rights and changes
Prefer original illustrations. Use photos only when they add evidence/context and rights are verified. Do not add reference footage to the public repository. The owner may change any default; the latest explicit owner edit takes precedence. Ask before materially redesigning the mascot, using unclear third-party assets, spending money or public release.
